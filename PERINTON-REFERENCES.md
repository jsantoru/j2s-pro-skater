# Perinton Skatepark: reference record

The level follows the completed park photographed in May 2024. The January 2023 master-plan rendering is an earlier concept and does **not** establish the final skate-feature layout. This is a playable reconstruction from photographs, not a surveyed digital twin.

## Sources and confidence

The [Town of Perinton park page](https://perinton.gov/departments/randp/parks-department/parks/perinton-skatepark/) identifies the address as 99 O’Connor Road and the skating area as **13,500 square feet** (about 1,254 square metres). The Town’s [opening announcement](https://perinton.gov/news/perinton-cuts-the-ribbon-on-highly-anticipated-skatepark/) records the May 18, 2024 opening. These are published facts; they do not supply precise feature dimensions or a construction coordinate system.

The [construction update](https://perinton.gov/news/perinton-skatepark-and-perinton-park-improvements-making-significant-progress/) and [site-work bid notice](https://perinton.gov/wp-content/uploads/Legal-Notice-Perinton-Park-Advertisement-for-Bids-5.26.23.pdf) establish the surrounding paths, lighting, furnishings, artificial turf and shade improvements. Spohn Ranch is identified as the skatepark design consultant. No dimensioned as-built skatepark drawing was found in the reviewed public material.

All six principal Town images and all thirteen visitor photographs were viewed directly during reconstruction. Local research copies are kept under `screenshots/perinton/references/` and are not game assets. Images elsewhere in the Town media library depicting Medina, Mattano, Niagara Falls or Oregon were excluded: those were precedent parks, not the completed Perinton park.

## Layout interpretation

Coordinates follow Grand Opening 33: **+X is image-right, +Z is toward the front entrance and parking**. This is a visual comparison frame, not a claim about geographic north. The approximately 44-by-40-metre outer envelope and all individual heights are estimates chosen from human-scale photo cues and the published area. The bounding rectangle includes turf and gaps; it is not itself the published skating area.

- The bowl is at the rear right. It has a larger deep pocket, a smaller shallow pocket, an inward pinch on both long sides, and a connecting floor slope. A continuous coping edge surrounds it; the photographs do not show an invented roll-in opening.
- The pump track is an **open serpentine ribbon**, with both ends returning to the plaza. Its western end doubles back through a short roller segment before the long rear straight. The eastern end turns through a broad bank and opens into the plaza. It is not a closed oval around the bowl.
- The central left feature is a substantial inverted-C / three-sided transition, open toward image-left. The near outer arm also forms a quarter facing the front plaza. It should read as a connected concrete volume with rounded inside corners, not three freestanding quarter-pipe boxes.
- The central street platform descends toward image-right through the **two-flat-two** stair set. The middle landing is visibly longer than either short tread. Straight galvanized rails flank it. The adjacent bank/A-frame has its own kinked ledge and rail profile.
- Both sides have low quarter walls; the eastern wall has two taller rectangular extensions. A mellow western bank/hip and a small rounded mound occupy the opposite side of the plaza. The entrance side includes a flatbar, open-bottom concrete benches/ledges, a low manual pad and a slappy curb.

The visitor lists quarter heights of approximately 3–5 feet and a 4-foot central transition. Those are helpful scale checks. The model’s bowl depths, exact radii, pump roller counts/heights and street riser dimensions remain estimates. The stair topology is visually established; its estimated 0.17-metre rise is not an official measurement.

### Bowl proportion audit

A second comparison with Grand Opening 33, the [visitor's bowl photograph 10](https://skatebuffalo.com/skateparks/perinton/images/10.jpg), and the construction aerial on printed page 18 of the [Town's 2023 annual report](https://perinton.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023-Perinton-Rec-Parks-Annual-Report_Reduced-Size.pdf) found the bowl compressed along its long axis. Its opening occupied about 27% of the modeled 44 m park width; the aerial suggests approximately 29–32%, with perspective uncertainty. The corrected plan extends the bowl and deck 20% along X about x=9, placing that ratio around 32%. Z dimensions, depth and transition runs are retained. This is a photo-informed proportion correction, not a newly verified measurement.

ROC City's existing 1.25 multiplier applies to both horizontal axes only. Reusing it throughout Perinton would add 56.25% to plan area; the published total area does not justify that adjustment. Likewise, published area alone cannot validate an individual feature. Joe measures approximately 1.756 m in both rendered worlds, so the perceived discrepancy was not an enlarged player model. No dimensioned bowl drawing was found in the additional audit.

## Materials and surroundings

The completed park uses tan/buff concrete on transition faces, bowl walls and many raised features; neutral pale-gray concrete on flat skating areas and bowl bottoms; and galvanized silver rails, coping and metal trim. ROC City’s blue/yellow trim palette does not belong here. Artificial-green islands surround the bowl and pump route, without invented interior trees blocking those lines.

The shade is a dark-green fabric canopy on silver posts near the front parking area, with green picnic tables outside the image-right edge. Black twin-head lights surround the park. Mature trees and the raised playground sit beyond image-left; modest buildings and utilities are visible beyond image-right. The [larger Perinton Park](https://perinton.gov/departments/randp/parks-department/parks/perinton-park/) connects to the Erie Canal and Canalway Trail, but the photographs do not put an exposed canal bank immediately against the skatepark.

## Review method

`sim/perintoncheck.js` records fixed, named aerial and ground camera coordinates for three separate review passes. Compare those renders with the completed-park photographs above. Geometry route tests and browser gameplay checks verify that the photo-derived transitions remain usable; they cannot prove surveyed accuracy. Any later dimensional claim should identify a stronger source before replacing these estimates.
